## Evidence

MSI's regulatory record identifies `MS-2651 / Raider 16 Max HX B2` with release date 14 January 2026. Retail evidence for the exact Belgian SKU reports an April 2026 launch. It is therefore a genuine 2026 model and chassis. Its Core Ultra 9 290HX Plus is nevertheless Arrow Lake Refresh/Core Ultra Series 2, not Panther Lake/Core Ultra Series 3; model year and CPU architecture must not be conflated.

The display lid/top cover is metal or aluminium, while the keyboard deck and underside are polycarbonate/plastic. Professional assessments differ in severity: HotHardware and Tom's Hardware found it well constructed and without excessive play, while TechRadar reported keyboard-deck flex and an exceptionally poor-feeling touchpad. PCWorld also called the underside cheap-feeling; GamesRadar reported hinge wobble and unreliable touchpad behaviour. This establishes a recurring input/finish criticism, not a recurring structural failure. No exact-generation hinge-breakage pattern was found.

The plastic construction does not invalidate cooling or performance and may reduce weight and surface heat, but it is below the mechanical/premium standard expected at this price. For a mostly stationary workstation with an external mouse it is a moderate disadvantage. For frequent travel over five to seven years it matters more because the 2.6 kg base repeatedly loads hinges, plastic screw bosses and deck. The exact chassis has existed for only months, so five-to-seven-year mechanical durability is UNKNOWN rather than PROVEN or disproven.

The glossy OLED is bright and accurately covers P3 in current measurements, but no exact-panel seven-year ageing evidence can exist yet. Static grandMA, CAD, editor and timeline interfaces create a credible cumulative uneven-wear/burn-in risk. MSI OLED Care mitigations do not make the panel burn-in-proof, and MSI's published three-year burn-in warranty applies to selected OLED monitors, not proof of equivalent Raider laptop coverage. For this use case OLED ageing and future panel availability are a larger lifecycle uncertainty than the plastic itself.
