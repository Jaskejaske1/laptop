## Evidence

Frandroid tested the 290HX Plus/RTX 5080 16GB configuration at its 175W maximum. It recorded Cinebench 2026 8980 multi/571 single, 3DMark Speed Way 5699 and Steel Nomad 5299, and explicitly reported that performance held over time. The reviewer found the thermal/noise profile unusually well controlled for the class, but measured only 3–4 hours of office use at 50% brightness.

Jagat Review confirms 175W GPU plus 125W CPU/300W combined operation, 32GB dual-channel RAM, 2TB plus a free M.2 slot, three fans, six heatpipes and five exhausts. Its reviewed panel measured 505-nit SDR, 1115-nit HDR and 99.6% DCI-P3. It is glossy OLED and MSI OLED Care is provided; that reduces but cannot eliminate long-term burn-in risk.

Notebookcheck's 5090 unit is same-current-chassis evidence rather than exact-GPU evidence. It measured 7h52 Wi-Fi, 26.6–26.9dB idle, roughly 51dB gaming, controlled surfaces, easy access to two SO-DIMMs/two M.2 slots and 1626us LatencyMon with no dropped frames. The CPU briefly reached 105C at very high short boost and stabilized near 96C at sustained high CPU power. This is not evidence of a recurring cooling defect, but it also does not prove every retail unit is defect-free.

Across the professional reviews inspected, no recurring crash, peripheral-dropout, firmware-bricking or cooling-failure pattern was identified for this 2026 chassis. That is affirmative review evidence of normal test stability, not an enterprise fleet-reliability guarantee.
