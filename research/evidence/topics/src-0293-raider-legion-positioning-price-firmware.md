## Findings

- MSI Raider 16 Max HX and Lenovo Legion Pro 7i 16 occupy the same high-performance 16-inch tier. The Legion Pro 7i is the more premium-built peer; Legion 9i is an 18-inch halo product closer to MSI Titan positioning.
- Belgian 2026 Pro 7i MTMs `83F500UAMB` and `83F500SKMB` are real, current, Belgian-AZERTY 290HX Plus/RTX 5080 175W configurations. No live Belgian price, stock or priced third year is proven.
- The German QWERTZ `83F500NPGE` at €3799 with three-year Premium Care is a useful regional market reference only. It is not a Belgian purchase price or eligible keyboard route.
- Legion 9i `83EY0031GM` is €5499 in an exact Greek/EU RTX 5080/64GB/4TB/three-year transaction. The Belgian page exposes a Belgian keyboard configuration but no usable live price. The family is therefore an over-budget reference, not an active candidate.
- Lenovo maintains a centralized platform download page with BIOS, VBIOS and driver packages and gives the Belgian 2026 Pro 7i MTMs an explicit PSREF end-of-support date of 2031-03-08. This is stronger lifecycle evidence than MSI's current consumer documentation, but it is not a seven-year firmware guarantee.
- Firmware is not spotless. Official BIOS `Q7CN78WW` remains distributed while isolated owners report NVMe/BSOD and audio regressions. Those reports are credible acceptance-test signals but do not establish prevalence or a structurally unstable Legion line. Professional loop testing independently shows strong sustained-performance stability; it does not validate every later BIOS release.

## Conclusion

The Legion Pro 7i has the stronger support/maintenance confidence and build than the Raider, but retains a shipping-BIOS acceptance gate. Exact Belgian price and third-year support—not platform capability—are now the main blockers.
