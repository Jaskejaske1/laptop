# Touchpad material is not the quality gate — SRC-0359

- https://www.dell.com/support/manuals/en-us/dell-pro-max-mb16250-laptop/dell-pro-max-16-plus-mb16250-owners-manual/touchpad?guid=guid-7157d7ae-610e-4b4a-a724-124cdf219190&lang=en-us

## Evidence

- Dell officially specifies the same 133×90mm, >300-DPI precision touchpad dimensions for Pro Max 16 Plus and Pro Max 18 Plus.
- Notebookcheck measured approximately 134×90mm and described the conventional clickpad as quiet and somewhat soft. TechRadar found the Pro Max 18 Plus pad responsive and easy to use.
- Dell's inspected manuals do not specify glass or Mylar. The buyer's physical Dell reference is not glass and nevertheless feels good. The supplied AI claim that Dell reserves premium glass pads exclusively for XPS is too broad and is not accepted as product evidence.
- Surface material is therefore descriptive only. Recommendation weight comes from area, placement, glide, tracking, palm rejection, click consistency and independent reviewer/user experience.

## Candidate impact

- Strix G16's approximately 150×100mm pad rises on buyer fit because professional reviews support its actual behavior; a likely-plastic surface is not a penalty by itself.
- Raider 16's approximately 130×83mm pad is not truly small versus Dell, but remains `CONFLICT` because reviewers disagree about tracking, hollowness, clicks and reliability.
- Legion Pro 7i remains disadvantaged primarily by its 120×75mm area and placement, not by the word `Mylar`.
- SCAR remains a positive input candidate because its large pad is consistently well reviewed, not merely because it is glass.
